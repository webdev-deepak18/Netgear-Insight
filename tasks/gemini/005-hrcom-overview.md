## Goal
Deepak has worked at HR.com for about 9 years. The NETGEAR hiring manager may ask "What does HR.com do?" He needs a clear, accurate 20-second answer and a few backup facts. HR.com runs several business lines, and he wants them mapped simply.

## What Deepak already knows (insider, treat as true)
- **Virtual events** are a themed set of several webcasts.
- **Sponsors** (HR tech vendors) fund research reports from the HR Research Institute. The findings are then presented at virtual events.
- **ePublications (ePubs)** are a separate content line.
- A first Gemini answer (from the Gemini app) only covered education, networking, research, compliance and events. **Don't repeat that general overview.** Add sourced facts and numbers.

## Questions to answer
1. In one or two sentences, what is HR.com? What is its main business, and who are its customers?
2. List each business line or product with one line on what it is and who it serves. Examples to check: membership/community, HR certification and education (HRCI/SHRM credits), HR Research Institute and reports, webcasts and virtual events, podcasts, publications/magazines, the job board or recruiting products, SaaS or software tools, and lead-generation or sponsorship for HR vendors.
3. How does HR.com make money? Which business lines bring in the revenue: sponsorship, memberships, certification, software?
4. Scale numbers HR.com states publicly, such as member count, webcasts per year or reports published. Give the date of each figure.
5. Any SaaS or software products, with a short description of each, especially recruiting or ATS-type tools. Also check whether "Nexthire" is connected to HR.com. A Nexthire explainer video is in Deepak's portfolio.
6. Basic company facts: founded when, headquarters, approximate company size, CEO.
7. Major changes in the last 3 years, such as new products, AI tools, acquisitions or rebrands.

## Scope & sources
- Use HR.com's own pages first: About, Media Kit or Advertise, the Membership pages, and the HR Research Institute.
- Then LinkedIn's company page, Crunchbase, and press releases.
- Use sources from the last 3 years for scale numbers.
- Skip employee reviews and salary data.

## Output format
- Bullets, scannable, max ~500 words.
- Every fact gets a working source URL. If you can't find a source, write "no source found". Never write a URL you haven't opened.
- Start with a **20-second spoken answer** to "What does HR.com do?" in plain words that a UX director outside HR tech would follow.
- End with "Implications for Deepak" (3–5 bullets): which business lines his UX work most likely touched, and how to link them to NETGEAR (B2B, dashboards, SaaS, subscriptions).
